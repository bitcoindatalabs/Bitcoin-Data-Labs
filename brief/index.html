Bitcoin has no annual report. So we publish one.

Bitcoin Data Labs measures Bitcoin in public. We turn its open record (every commit and code review, every Lightning channel, every block) into datasets, dashboards, reports and research papers that anyone can check, on a fixed schedule and with no paywall.

Why it matters

Bitcoin has no company behind it: no CEO, no board, no earnings call. Everything about it is public, from the code review behind each release to the channels of its Lightning payment network, but the record is scattered across GitHub, mailing lists, chat logs, network gossip and the chain itself.

So debates about software clients, centralization and funding run on opinion. We assemble the numbers once, with published methods, and keep them current for everyone who works on Bitcoin or reports on it.

  • Developers and maintainers: review load, maintainer risk and who contributes to Bitcoin Core.
  • Node operators and implementers: which software Lightning runs, and how fast it upgrades.
  • Funders: where development effort and grant money go.
  • Journalists and researchers: citable numbers with the source, the method and the code.

Stories the data can support

  1. Is Bitcoin Core development speeding up?Monthly merges, review health and new contributors.
  2. Who pays Bitcoin's developers, and for what?Funders, grantees and the projects they support.
  3. Does Lightning depend on one piece of software?Paper 01: what the 2026 Core Lightning shutdown showed.
  4. Do node operators patch after an advisory?Paper 02: the release line of every public node, 2023–2026.
  5. How concentrated is Bitcoin mining?Every block since genesis attributed to its pool.
  6. What happens when wallet randomness is weak?1,983 BTC swept from 4,925 addresses in 22 hours.

Where we're going

Our aim is a public annual report for Bitcoin: the same measured, reproducible record for every major implementation, every second layer and the chain itself, kept current and free to use.

  • Research. Next in the series: what a force close costs, and how channels migrate if legacy signatures retire.
  • Coverage. More implementations and Lightning clients, and more layers after Liquid.
  • Reach. Every paper paired with a plain-language write-up for the people who run the software.
